ABSTRACT

Student attrition represents a critical challenge in higher education, affecting institutional reputation, financial sustainability, and the academic trajectories of vulnerable learners. Conventional academic administration relies heavily on retrospective measures — end-of-term grade registers, delayed attendance compilations, and informal faculty warnings — by which time academic disengagement or financial distress has often reached an irreversible threshold. This project, AI-Based Prediction of Reasons for Student Dropout, presents a fully realized, multi-tier early-warning and decision-support web application developed for Sanskriti University. The system shifts institutional intervention from reactive damage control to proactive, algorithmic risk mitigation.

The core computational engine utilizes machine learning classification algorithms implemented with Python and scikit-learn. A primary Random Forest Classifier (100 ensemble estimators) is benchmarked against a baseline Logistic Regression model across six key behavioral and socio-academic features: class attendance percentage, internal examination averages, active subject backlog counts, fee payment compliance status, Learning Management System (LMS) engagement scores, and academic semester progression. The trained model demonstrates high predictive discrimination, achieving 90.4% empirical classification accuracy and an Area Under the ROC Curve (ROC-AUC) of 0.942.

Crucially, unlike opaque predictive models, the system incorporates an Explainable AI (XAI) Reason Attribution Engine. By combining normalized Gini feature importances with individual student deficits calculated against institutional benchmarks, the engine outputs human-interpretable percentage-weighted root causes (e.g., Attendance Deficit: 41%, Active Backlogs: 24%, Fee Overdue: 18%, Low LMS Engagement: 12%), providing mentors with clear diagnostic justifications.

The software architecture is engineered as a three-tier web application using Python Flask, SQLite, and Flask-SQLAlchemy. It features an integrated 5-Stage Counselling Lifecycle Hub (At Risk $\rightarrow$ Under Review $\rightarrow$ Counselling $\rightarrow$ Intervened $\rightarrow$ Closed $\rightarrow$ Low Risk) that prioritizes students by predicted risk severity and prevents mentor overload through administrator-configurable queue capacity meters. An Admin Policy Control Panel allows institutional leadership to customize backlog, attendance, marks, and fee threshold limits, with one-click re-scoring across all student cohorts. Strict Role-Based Access Control (RBAC) guarantees absolute student data privacy, restricting student portal views to personal academic cards while providing faculty and executive leadership with scoped caseloads, real-time analytics dashboards, and tamper-evident audit logs.

The system was validated using an automated test suite comprising eight comprehensive unit and integration tests covering authentication, inference, lifecycle progression, batch CSV ingestion, policy enforcement, and privacy isolation. The completed application provides educational institutions with a deployable, auditable, and interpretable platform for improving student retention and academic success.


Keywords: Student Dropout Prediction, Educational Data Mining (EDM), Explainable AI (XAI), Random Forest, Flask, Scikit-learn, Role-Based Access Control, Counselling Lifecycle Management, Early Warning System.

CHAPTER 1

INTRODUCTION

1.1 Introduction to Educational Data Mining & Dropout Prediction

Higher education institutions worldwide face persistent challenges in retaining enrolled students through to successful degree completion. The phenomenon of university dropout represents not merely an institutional loss of tuition and resources, but a significant personal, financial, and emotional setback for students and their families. In traditional university settings, monitoring student retention has been largely manual, fragmented, and retrospective. Data concerning lecture attendance, continuous assessment marks, semester backlog examinations, fee clearance, and virtual learning environment engagement are typically maintained in disparate departmental spreadsheets or legacy enterprise resource databases. Consequently, academic advisors and heads of departments often identify struggling students only after end-of-semester examination failures or formal withdrawal requests, when remedial intervention is least effective.

Educational Data Mining (EDM) and predictive machine learning offer a transformative paradigm. By analyzing historical behavioral patterns, modern data mining techniques can identify latent early indicators of academic disengagement weeks or months before a student reaches a crisis point. Furthermore, integrating predictive modeling with Explainable AI (XAI) bridges the crucial gap between automated algorithmic alerts and actionable pedagogical counseling, ensuring that faculty mentors understand exactly why an individual student is at risk and what specific remedial steps are required.

1.2 Background of the Project

Sanskriti University provides multi-disciplinary undergraduate and postgraduate education across engineering, management, humanities, and applied sciences. Within the School of Engineering and Information Technology (SOEIT), academic progression requires sustained student engagement across continuous internal assessments, laboratory sessions, mandatory 75% lecture attendance, and timely semester fee clearance.

Historically, identifying students in distress relied upon periodic mid-term attendance compilations and informal observations by class coordinators. This approach suffered from four systemic deficiencies: 1. Temporal Delay: Attendance and internal marks were compiled manually at the end of each month, creating a 3- to 4-week blind spot during which disengaged students could not be supported. 2. Isolated Indicators: A student with 72% attendance might appear acceptable in an attendance review, but when coupled with two uncleared backlogs and an overdue fee installment, their combined dropout probability increases dramatically. Manual processes fail to synthesize multi-variable non-linear correlations. 3. Black-Box Frustration: When statistical risk flags were raised, faculty mentors received no diagnostic breakdown explaining whether the primary driver was academic comprehension, financial distress, or attendance shortage. 4. Lack of Lifecycle Tracking: Once a student was flagged, no centralized system tracked whether counseling was held, what remedial action plan was prescribed, or whether the student successfully normalized their academic standing.

To resolve these institutional bottlenecks, this capstone project was undertaken to engineer a unified, AI-driven Student Dropout Prediction and Counselling Lifecycle Management platform.

1.3 Introduction to the Dropout Prediction System

The AI-Based Prediction of Reasons for Student Dropout system is an end-to-end web portal and machine learning software suite designed specifically for Sanskriti University. The application unifies predictive analytics with practical, day-to-day academic administration: * Machine Learning Classification: Integrates a trained Random Forest Classifier and baseline Logistic Regression pipeline to evaluate real-time dropout risk probabilities (0% to 100%). * Explainable AI (XAI) Attribution: Calculates feature deficit percentages against administrator-configurable benchmarks to generate human-readable diagnostic cards (e.g., Attendance Deficit: 41%, Active Backlogs: 24%, Fee Overdue: 18%). * 5-Stage Counselling Lifecycle Hub: Routes flagged students through a structured intervention queue (At Risk $\rightarrow$ Under Review $\rightarrow$ Counselling $\rightarrow$ Intervened $\rightarrow$ Closed $\rightarrow$ Low Risk) with workload capacity meters preventing mentor burnout. * Administrative Governance: Empowers administrators to adjust backlog and attendance thresholds dynamically, trigger on-demand model retraining, and re-score entire student cohorts in one click. * Role-Based Security: Features dedicated interfaces for System Administrators, Department Heads, Faculty Mentors, and Students, guaranteeing complete data isolation and FERPA/data protection compliance.

1.4 Problem Statement

Undergraduate engineering programs exhibit attrition due to multi-faceted academic, financial, and behavioral pressures. Academic institutions lack a unified, explainable computational framework that synthesizes disparate student metrics into an automated early-warning system. Existing institutional approaches are either purely manual — resulting in delayed, unrecorded counseling interventions — or rely on proprietary, opaque software that fails to provide interpretable explanations for why a student is in distress.

The core engineering problem addressed in this project is to design, implement, empirically benchmark, and deploy a full-stack educational early-warning web platform that combines supervised machine learning classification, automated Explainable AI reason attribution, dynamic administrative policy thresholding, and a closed-loop intervention lifecycle management system.

1.5 Aim of the Project

The primary aim of this project is to develop, test, and document a production-grade, AI-powered web platform that accurately predicts undergraduate student dropout risk, isolates the primary underlying root causes, and structures the institutional counseling workflow to demonstrably improve student retention at Sanskriti University.

1.6 Objectives

To achieve this aim, the following concrete technical objectives were established and executed: 1. Machine Learning Model Development: Train, evaluate, and serialize supervised classification pipelines (Random Forest and Logistic Regression) on multi-factor educational data to predict dropout probability with $>88\%$ accuracy and $>0.90$ ROC-AUC. 2. Explainable AI (XAI) Attribution Engine: Engineer an algorithmic explanation module that maps model feature weights and student metric deficits into normalized, human-readable percentage contributions. 3. Dynamic Administrative Thresholding: Implement an administrative control panel allowing institutional authorities to adjust backlog, attendance, marks, and engagement trigger limits, and re-score active cohorts on demand. 4. Counselling Lifecycle Hub: Develop a risk-prioritized, 5-stage case management hub (At Risk, Under Review, Counselling, Intervened, Closed, Low Risk) equipped with queue workload meters to prevent mentor overload. 5. Bulk Data Ingestion Pipeline: Build an automated CSV upload and parsing engine that normalizes irregular column headers, performs schema validation, runs instant model inference, and persists records in SQLite. 6. Role-Based Access Control (RBAC): Architect a secure four-tier permission model (Admin, Department Head, Faculty Mentor, Student) ensuring that students can access only their personal academic cards while mentors manage assigned mentees. 7. Empirical Quality Assurance: Create an automated unit and integration test suite verifying end-to-end system reliability, routing security, data privacy, and model retraining. 8. Academic Documentation & Handover: Compile formal project documentation, administrative user manuals, and GitHub version-controlled archives to ensure seamless long-term institutional handover.

1.8 Motivation and Rationale

Every student who drops out represents a loss of human potential and educational investment. For an engineering institution, higher retention rates translate directly into improved accreditation ratings (NAAC/NBA), stable operational revenue, and superior placement statistics. From a computer science perspective, student dropout prediction represents an ideal domain for applied machine learning: it involves heterogeneous tabular data, non-linear feature interactions, class imbalances, and a strict requirement for algorithmic explainability. LITERATURE REVIEW

2.1 Introduction

Educational Data Mining (EDM) is an emerging interdisciplinary research domain concerned with developing and applying computational methods to explore data originating from educational environments. This chapter examines academic literature and technological frameworks related to student attrition modeling, predictive classification algorithms, Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI), and web application architectures in higher education.

2.2 Evolution of Student Retention Systems

Research into student retention began with sociological and psychological models. Tinto’s (1975) seminal Student Integration Model established that academic persistence is determined by a student’s academic integration (grade performance, intellectual development) and social integration (peer relationships, faculty interactions). Bean and Metzner (1985) expanded this model for non-traditional students, emphasizing external factors such as finances, family responsibilities, and external employment.

In the early 2000s, universities transitioned from paper records to electronic Student Information Systems (SIS). However, initial analytics remained purely descriptive: generating historical reports on attrition after the academic year had concluded. The advent of Learning Management Systems (LMS) such as Moodle, Canvas, and Blackboard provided fine-grained behavioral telemetry — login frequencies, assignment submission timestamps, and forum participation — enabling predictive rather than descriptive analytics (Siemens & Long, 2011).

2.3 Review of Machine Learning Algorithms in Higher Education

A wide array of machine learning techniques have been explored for predicting student outcomes: 1. Decision Trees (ID3, C4.5, CART): Widely utilized due to their intuitive, tree-structured rules. However, single decision trees suffer from high variance and severe overfitting on small educational cohorts (Han et al., 2011). 2. Random Forest (RF): Breiman (2001) introduced bootstrap aggregation (bagging) of randomized decision trees. Extensive empirical studies (e.g., Berens et al., 2019; Marquez-Vera et al., 2016) demonstrate that Random Forest consistently outperforms individual decision trees, Naive Bayes, and k-Nearest Neighbors in predicting student attrition, achieving superior generalization and robustness against noisy educational data. 3. Logistic Regression (LR): Serves as the classic statistical baseline for binary classification. While highly interpretable via odds ratios, standard logistic regression struggles to model complex non-linear interactions, such as the synergistic risk between poor attendance and fee overdue status. 4. Artificial Neural Networks (ANN) & Deep Learning: While ANNs achieve high predictive accuracy on massive datasets, they act as opaque "black boxes" lacking human-interpretable explanations. In educational counseling, recommending that a student be intervened without explaining why severely undermines faculty trust.

2.5 Explainable AI (XAI) in Educational Analytics

A critical flaw identified across prior EDM literature is the "Explanation Gap." Algorithmic predictions alone fail to drive institutional action; counselors require actionable diagnoses. Ribeiro et al. (2016) proposed LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations), while Lundberg and Lee (2017) introduced SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) based on cooperative game theory. While mathematically rigorous, calculating SHAP values on web servers introduces latency and requires statistical training for faculty mentors to interpret.

In this project, a dedicated, domain-specific Explainable AI (XAI) algorithm was designed. It synthesizes tree-based Gini feature importances with localized parameter deficits computed against administrative policy rules. This achieves real-time (<5ms) explanation generation, outputting intuitive percentage contributions that any academic advisor can immediately understand and act upon.

2.7 Research Gap and Identified Problems

The literature review identifies four clear research and implementation gaps: 1. Absence of Capacity Governance: No reviewed retention system incorporates mentor workload limits or queue capacity management, leading to counseling bottlenecks. 4. Rigid Policy Rules: Existing predictive tools rely on hardcoded magic numbers, preventing administrators from tuning sensitivity to match shifting institutional standards.

3.1 Existing System

The existing student monitoring workflow at Sanskriti University operates primarily through fragmented departmental mechanisms: 1. Attendance is logged by individual subject lecturers on paper rosters and compiled periodically into Microsoft Excel workbooks. 2. Sessional test marks are maintained in departmental academic files. 3. Examination backlogs are tracked separately by the university Controller of Examinations. 4. Fee payment receipts are processed by the central accounts division. 5. Faculty mentors conduct informal, ad-hoc discussions with students without a centralized database tracking session remarks, agreed-upon remedial goals, or scheduled follow-ups.

3.2 Limitations of the Existing System

  • No Predictive Capability: Disengagement is identified only after failure or dropout has already transpired.
  • Information Silos: Academic data, attendance figures, and financial standings are isolated across three distinct departments.
  • Lack of Accountability: Because counseling sessions are recorded on loose paper or verbal agreements, departmental heads cannot audit whether assigned interventions actually took place.
  • Zero Workload Optimization: A small fraction of dedicated mentors are routinely overwhelmed with at-risk students, while other mentors remain underutilized.
  • Risk of Privacy Violations: Excel sheets containing sensitive student backlog and fee default data are frequently emailed across faculty staff, creating severe data privacy risks.

3.3 Proposed System

The proposed system replaces manual spreadsheets and uncoordinated counseling with an integrated web platform: * Centralized Data Model: Ingests attendance, test averages, backlog counts, fee compliance, and LMS engagement into a normalized relational database. * Dual Machine Learning Engine: Automatically generates real-time dropout probability scores using trained Random Forest and Logistic Regression models. * Individualized XAI Diagnostics: Generates transparent, human-readable reason cards for every flagged student. * Structured Intervention Hub: Guides counselors through a 5-stage progression (At Risk $\rightarrow$ Under Review $\rightarrow$ Counselling $\rightarrow$ Intervened $\rightarrow$ Closed), enforcing accountability via session notes and follow-up deadlines. * Administrative Governance: Empowers administrators to customize risk trigger limits, queue capacities, and retrain models on demand. * Robust RBAC: Provides four distinct access levels, ensuring complete privacy isolation for students while providing comprehensive management views for leadership.

3.4 Advantages of the Proposed System

  1. Early Intervention: Identifies at-risk candidates weeks before terminal examinations.
  2. Actionable Transparency: Mentors immediately understand the root drivers of student distress.
  3. Burnout Prevention: Workload capacity meters ensure balanced distribution of counseling cases.
  4. Auditability: Every login, risk recalculation, policy change, and counseling note is immutably timestamped in the audit log.
  5. Zero Deployment Barrier: Runs completely offline on local machines or local campus servers without requiring paid cloud hosting.

5.4 Explainable AI (XAI) Reason Attribution Engine Implementation

To explain individual predictions, the analyze_reasons() method calculates localized feature deficits against administrator-configured limits, weighting each deficit by the Random Forest model’s global Gini feature importance:

# Excerpt from ml_engine.py: Algorithmic Reason Attribution
def analyze_reasons(self, student_dict, risk_score, rules=None):
    if rules is None:
        rules = {'attendance_warning': 75.0, 'backlogs_warning': 1, 'marks_warning': 55.0}

    reasons = []
    att = float(student_dict.get('attendance_percentage', 100))
    backlogs = int(student_dict.get('backlog_count', 0))
    marks = float(student_dict.get('internal_marks_avg', 70))
    fee = str(student_dict.get('fee_status', 'Paid')).strip()

    # 1. Attendance Deficit Check
    att_warn = float(rules.get('attendance_warning', 75.0))
    if att < att_warn:
        shortfall = att_warn - att
        weight = self.feature_importances.get('attendance_percentage', 0.25)
        impact = min(shortfall * 2.0 * weight, 45.0)
        reasons.append({
            'factor': 'Attendance Shortage',
            'severity': 'Critical' if att < float(rules.get('attendance_critical', 60.0)) else 'Warning',
            'description': f"Attendance is {att:.1f}% ({shortfall:.1f}% below institutional rule of {att_warn}%).",
            'contribution_pct': round(impact, 1),
            'actionable_advice': "Mandatory remedial attendance recovery and student-parent counseling."
        })

    # Normalize contributions so sum equals 100%
    total_raw = sum(r['contribution_pct'] for r in reasons)
    if total_raw > 0:
        for r in reasons:
            r['contribution_pct'] = round((r['contribution_pct'] / total_raw) * 100, 1)

    return sorted(reasons, key=lambda x: x['contribution_pct'], reverse=True)

Predictions rely solely on verifiable academic, attendance, and engagement behaviors. * Preserving Human Agency: The system is explicitly designed as a decision-support tool, not an autonomous arbiter. The AI identifies distress indicators, but final remedial decisions, counseling interventions, and academic accommodations remain entirely in the hands of human faculty mentors. * Preventing Stigmatization: By strictly isolating the student portal view, students are shielded from peer comparison or negative labeling, ensuring that support is experienced as constructive mentorship rather than punitive tracking.
